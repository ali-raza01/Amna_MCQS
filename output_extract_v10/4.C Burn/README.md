### Q1 — Syncope 24 hours after major thermal burn
**Question:** Syncope during positioning/edema management 24 hours after major thermal burn is most likely due to?

**Chosen answer:** **3 — intravascular hypovolemia**

**Confidence:** High

**Reasoning:**
- Major burns cause massive plasma loss into the interstitium and burn edema, producing burn shock/hypovolemia in the first 24–48 hours.
- Orthostatic hypotension can occur with positioning, but the underlying cause is intravascular volume depletion.
- Pain and infection are less likely to be the primary cause this early.

**Self-check:** Pass.

### Q2 — Burn affecting epidermis and superficial dermis
**Question:** Burn involving epidermis and superficial part of dermis.

**Chosen answer:** **1 — superficial partial thickness**

**Confidence:** High

**Reasoning:**
- Superficial partial-thickness burns involve the epidermis and superficial (papillary) dermis.
- Deep partial thickness extends into deeper dermis; full thickness extends through the dermis.

**Self-check:** Pass.

### Q3 — Anesthetic burn with eschar and subcutaneous damage
**Question:** Burn with anesthetic wound, significant eschar, and moderate subcutaneous tissue damage.

**Chosen answer:** **4 — Full thickness**

**Confidence:** Medium

**Reasoning:**
- Anesthesia and eschar strongly suggest full-thickness injury.
- Subcutaneous tissue involvement also indicates full-thickness burn.
- Deep partial-thickness burns may have decreased sensation but are less typically anesthetic.

**Self-check:** Pass.

### Q4 — Incorrect shoulder/pectoral burn management
**Question:** Which is incorrect about burn in shoulder and pectoral region?

**Chosen answer:** **3 — put in flex add int rot**

**Confidence:** High

**Reasoning:**
- Axillary/shoulder burns are positioned in abduction and external rotation to prevent adduction/internal rotation contracture.
- Flexion, adduction, and internal rotation promote deformity and are incorrect.

**Self-check:** Pass.

### Q5 — Shoulder/arm burn: avoid
**Question:** Burn at shoulder and arm — avoid?

**Chosen answer:** **3 — Adduction & internal rotation**

**Confidence:** High

**Reasoning:**
- Adduction/internal rotation predisposes to axillary contracture.
- Abduction and airplane splinting are used preventively.

**Self-check:** Pass.

### Q11 — False initial post-burn change
**Question:** Which statement is false about initial changes after burn?

**Chosen answer:** **4 — This patient initially experienced a decrease in fibrinogen**

**Confidence:** High

**Reasoning:**
- Fibrinogen is an acute-phase reactant and typically increases after burn, not decreases.
- Hemoconcentration can increase RBC count; WBC and free fatty acids also rise.

### Q13 — Full-thickness right lower extremity burn: prevent contracture
**Question:** Movements to stress with splinting/positioning/exercise.

### Q15 — Black eschar over 90% of wound bed: first/best treatment
**Question:** Best and first treatment for black eschar.

**Chosen answer:** **4 — Elase**

**Confidence:** Medium

**Reasoning:**
- Black eschar requires debridement; Elase is an enzymatic debriding agent.
- Silvadene is antimicrobial, not a debriding agent.
- Surgical debridement would be definitive but is not listed.

**Self-check:** Pass.

### Q16 — Leukocyte response after emigration
**Question:** Next response of leukocytes after emigration into tissues during acute inflammation.

**Chosen answer:** **4 — Engulfment**

**Confidence:** High

**Reasoning:**
- Sequence: margination → pavementing → adhesion → emigration → chemotaxis → phagocytosis/engulfment.
- After emigration, the next major response listed is engulfment.

**Self-check:** Pass.
